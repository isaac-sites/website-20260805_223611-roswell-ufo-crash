## Introduction

Only two known surviving federal records created in 1947 directly address the [Roswell incident]({{ 'the-1980-book/' | relative_url }}): a July 1947 historical report from the combined 509th Bomb Group and [Roswell Army Air]({{ 'roswell-army-air/' | relative_url }}) Field, and an FBI teletype dated 8 July 1947. Their rarity makes them disproportionately important. Rather than providing independent accounts of an unidentified spacecraft, both documents describe the recovery of debris that military authorities associated with a balloon or radar-tracking equipment. At the same time, they were produced for different institutional purposes, contain different levels of detail, and carry different evidentiary weight. Understanding where these records agree—and where they differ—is essential for assessing what the surviving contemporary government record can and cannot establish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

## What the Two Records Agree On

Despite their different origins, the two documents share several important points.

Both records indicate that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Military personnel recovered physical debris connected with the Roswell incident.
* The event attracted attention beyond the local base, involving more than a routine internal matter.
* The recovered material was ultimately described in balloon-related terms rather than as an unknown craft.
* Neither document mentions extraterrestrial technology, recovered bodies, biological remains or any secret programme to exploit alien materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

</div>

This agreement is significant because the documents were created independently by different federal organisations. The military history was an internal administrative record summarising activities at Roswell Army Air Field, while the FBI teletype recorded information passed from military officials to Bureau personnel. Their shared conclusion does not prove the balloon explanation was correct, but it does show that, within days of the incident, separate federal records were describing the debris in substantially similar terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

## Differences in Wording and Institutional Purpose

The similarities between the documents should not obscure their distinct functions.

### The military historical report

The monthly history of the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field was intended to document noteworthy activities at the installation. Its Roswell entry is brief. It records that the base became involved in recovering a "[flying disc]({{ 'flying-disc/' | relative_url }})" before noting that military authorities later identified the object as a radar-tracking balloon. The wording preserves both stages of the episode: the initial characterisation and the subsequent official determination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

Because the report was compiled as part of the base's historical record rather than as an investigative file, it provides little descriptive detail about the recovered material or how the conclusion was reached.

### The FBI teletype

The FBI message served a different purpose. Sent from the Bureau's Dallas office on 8 July 1947, it relayed information reportedly received from an Eighth Air Force official. Unlike the military history, the teletype includes physical characteristics of the object, describing it as hexagonal in shape, suspended beneath a large balloon by cable and resembling a high-altitude weather balloon fitted with a radar reflector. It also noted that the object was reportedly being forwarded to Wright Field for examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/1995-07-28-gao-roswell-report-nsiad-95-187-gao-nsiad-95-187-roswell-1995" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Transparency GAO Report to the Honorable Steven H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Schiff, Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico (NSIAD-95-187), U.S. Gener...</span></span></span>

Importantly, the teletype is not an FBI forensic investigation. It records what military personnel communicated to FBI officials rather than observations made independently by Bureau agents. Its evidentiary value therefore lies in documenting what federal agencies were saying at the time, not in independently verifying the object's identity.

## Comparing Their Evidentiary Weight

The two documents complement one another but should not be treated as equally strong forms of [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}).

The military history is a contemporaneous official record created by the organisation directly involved in the recovery. Although concise, it represents an institutional summary prepared as part of routine record-keeping.

The FBI teletype is contemporaneous as well, but it is one step removed from the underlying events. It documents reported information transmitted between agencies. Historians therefore distinguish between the teletype as evidence of official communication and direct evidence of the recovered object itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because later discussions sometimes cite the FBI document as though it represented an independent Bureau investigation. It does not. Instead, it shows that the FBI recorded the military's explanation during the first day of national attention surrounding the incident.

## How Much Corroboration Do Two Documents Provide?

The existence of two contemporaneous records offers meaningful, but limited, corroboration.

The documents reinforce one another in several respects:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* They independently confirm that a recovery operation occurred.
* They both connect the recovered material with balloon-related equipment.

However, their agreement should not be overstated.

Neither document explains precisely what equipment was recovered, identifies all personnel involved, or provides technical analysis of the debris. Neither includes photographs, laboratory reports or engineering evaluations. They therefore corroborate a broad narrative—that debris was recovered and officially characterised as balloon-related—but they do not independently establish every factual detail of that explanation.

The General Accounting Office's 1995 search is important in this context. After examining numerous government archives and classified collections, it reported finding only these two surviving federal records from 1947 that directly addressed the Roswell incident. It also found no contemporaneous federal records supporting later claims involving recovered spacecraft or non-human bodies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

## What These Records Can—and Cannot—Resolve

The two surviving 1947 records establish a narrow but reliable documentary foundation.

They demonstrate that the Roswell incident generated genuine official attention in July 1947, that military personnel recovered debris, and that the surviving contemporaneous federal record consistently described the recovered material in balloon-related terms. At the same time, their brevity leaves many questions unanswered, including the exact chain of custody, the technical assessment process and the extent of documentation that may once have existed but has not survived.

As a result, these documents neither conclusively settle every later debate about Roswell nor provide evidence for the more elaborate claims that emerged decades afterwards. Their principal historical value lies in offering two independent snapshots of what federal agencies recorded at the time, allowing later interpretations to be measured against the small body of surviving contemporaneous evidence rather than against retrospective testimony alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>
