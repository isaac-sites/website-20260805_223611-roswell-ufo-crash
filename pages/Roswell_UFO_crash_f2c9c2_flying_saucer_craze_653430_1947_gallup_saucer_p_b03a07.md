## Introduction

The first nationwide Gallup poll on flying saucers, conducted in August 1947, captured a revealing moment in the early history of the UFO phenomenon. Coming only weeks after Kenneth Arnold's sighting and the [Roswell incident]({{ 'the-1980-book/' | relative_url }}), the survey showed that the flying saucer story had spread with extraordinary speed across the United States. Yet it also demonstrated something often overlooked in later retellings: widespread awareness did not translate into widespread belief that the objects were extraterrestrial spacecraft. Instead, most Americans either had no firm opinion or favoured ordinary explanations such as optical illusions, hoaxes or secret military technology. This snapshot of public opinion provides an important benchmark for understanding how the [flying saucer craze]({{ 'the-1940-s-flying/' | relative_url }}) differed from the later, more familiar association between UFOs and alien visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/gallup.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[project1947.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - August 1947 Gallup Poll...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1947</span></span></span>

## How widely the saucer story had spread

The Gallup poll found that approximately **90% of Americans had heard of flying saucers**. Considering that Kenneth Arnold's report had occurred only in late June 1947 and Roswell had entered newspaper headlines in early July, this level of public recognition was remarkable. Gallup itself noted that awareness of flying saucers exceeded awareness of several major political issues of the day, illustrating how quickly newspapers and radio had transformed a handful of sightings into a national conversation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/gallup.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - August 1947 Gallup Poll...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1947</span></span></span>

The result also demonstrates the power of post-war mass media. National wire services, local newspapers and radio broadcasts repeatedly covered new sightings, giving Americans across the country a common vocabulary. By August, "flying saucer" had become a widely recognised term even though no official explanation had emerged. Historians of [UFO culture]({{ 'roswell-and-ufo/' | relative_url }}) often identify this rapid spread as one of the defining characteristics of [the 1947 flying]({{ 'the-1947-flying/' | relative_url }}) saucer craze.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.historians.org/perspectives-article/more-human-than-alien-researching-the-history-of-ufos-march-2017/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historians.org">[AHA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historians.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AHAMore Human Than Alien – AHA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AHAMore Human Than Alien – AHA</span></span></span>

## Which explanations Americans preferred

The Gallup survey did not ask whether flying saucers existed. Instead, it asked respondents what they believed the reported objects actually were. The distribution of answers paints a much more cautious picture than later popular mythology suggests.

The principal responses were:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **33%** had no opinion or did not know.
* **29%** believed witnesses had seen optical illusions, mirages or products of imagination.
* **15%** thought the objects were secret American weapons or military projects.
* **10%** believed the reports were hoaxes.
* **3%** suggested weather-related devices.
* **1%** believed they were Soviet weapons.
* **2%** offered explanations involving searchlights on aircraft.
* **9%** gave miscellaneous answers, ranging from biblical signs to effects connected with atomic testing or other speculative ideas. Because some respondents gave more than one answer, the totals exceed 100%.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/gallup.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - August 1947 Gallup Poll...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1947</span></span></span>

</div>

One striking feature of these results is the absence of a significant extraterrestrial category. Any respondents who imagined visitors from space were grouped within the small and varied "other" category rather than representing a notable public consensus. Contemporary historians therefore caution against projecting later UFO beliefs back onto the summer of 1947.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/ufos-uapswhatever-we-call-them-why-do-we-assume-mysterious-flying-objects-are-extraterrestrial-180978374/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[Smithsonian Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on smithsonianmag.com.</span></span></span>

## Why awareness did not equal alien belief

The poll illustrates that Americans in 1947 interpreted mysterious aerial reports through the concerns of their own era rather than through an established alien narrative.

Several factors shaped these expectations:

* **[Military secrecy]({{ 'military-secrecy/' | relative_url }}).** The Second World War had recently ended, and many advanced weapons had remained secret until deployment. Assuming the objects were experimental American aircraft was therefore a plausible explanation.
* **Cold War anxieties.** Although only a small proportion blamed the Soviet Union, fears about foreign technology had already begun to influence public thinking.
* **Scepticism toward sensational stories.** Nearly four in ten respondents either attributed the reports to illusion or outright hoaxes, showing that many people viewed the craze with considerable caution.
* **Lack of an established extraterrestrial narrative.** The idea that flying saucers were alien spacecraft had not yet become culturally dominant. That interpretation gained much greater visibility only over the following several years through books, magazine articles and popular culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/ufos-uapswhatever-we-call-them-why-do-we-assume-mysterious-flying-objects-are-extraterrestrial-180978374/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on smithsonianmag.com.</span></span></span>

This distinction is historically important. Modern discussions often assume that the Roswell story immediately produced widespread beliefs about crashed alien craft. The Gallup [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) shows otherwise: in mid-1947, Americans were generally trying to explain reports within familiar frameworks of military technology, atmospheric phenomena or mistaken observation.

## What the poll reveals about the 1947 flying saucer craze

Because it was conducted during the height of the original saucer wave, the Gallup survey serves as one of the best quantitative snapshots of public opinion before UFO culture evolved into its later form.

The findings reveal three important points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The flying saucer phenomenon spread faster than agreement about its meaning.** Nearly everyone had heard the stories, but there was no consensus about what they represented.
* **Most explanations remained Earth-bound.** Secret weapons, illusions and hoaxes comfortably outweighed more extraordinary interpretations.
* **Public uncertainty was itself significant.** One-third of respondents simply did not know what to think, leaving space for competing explanations to develop during the following years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/gallup.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[project1947.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - August 1947 Gallup Poll...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1947</span></span></span>

</div>

Subsequent books, media coverage and alleged UFO encounters increasingly promoted the extraterrestrial hypothesis, but the August 1947 Gallup poll demonstrates that this was not the prevailing public view at the beginning of the modern UFO era. Rather than showing a nation convinced of alien visitors, it records a population that was highly aware of the flying saucer reports yet largely undecided or inclined toward conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/ufos-uapswhatever-we-call-them-why-do-we-assume-mysterious-flying-objects-are-extraterrestrial-180978374/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: smithsonianmag.com">[smithsonianmag.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">smithsonianmag.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on smithsonianmag.com.</span></span></span>

    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
