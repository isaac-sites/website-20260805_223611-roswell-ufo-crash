## Introduction

Roswell’s annual UFO Festival is often described as attracting anything from a few thousand people to tens of thousands, leading many visitors to wonder whether the figures contradict one another. In reality, they usually measure different things under different circumstances. Festival attendance is not a simple audited gate count. Instead, the City of Roswell publishes estimates based on the scale of the event, visitor surveys, ticket sales, tourism modelling and economic-impact calculations. Anniversary years, changes in programming, marketing and counting methods can therefore produce dramatic swings that reflect genuine differences in the festival as well as differences in how success is measured.

## The exceptional scale of the 2022 anniversary

The largest recent jump came in 2022, when Roswell marked the 75th anniversary of the 1947 incident. The city deliberately expanded the festival with larger concerts, additional attractions, comic-con programming, tours and outdoor events designed to attract visitors from well beyond southeastern New Mexico.

## Why the 2023 estimate was much lower

The following year illustrates why comparisons can be misleading.

For the 76th anniversary in 2023, the city reported:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="#endnote-5" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: For the 76th anniversary in 2023, the city reported:">[For the 76th anniversary in 2023, the city reported:]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">For the 76th anniversary in 2023, the city reported:</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

* approximately **3,370 visitors**;
* about **$510,000 in direct economic impact**.

At first glance, this appears to represent a collapse of more than 90% from 2022. However, the city's own report shows that the 2023 event was organised very differently.

Rather than reproducing the large-scale anniversary programme, organisers shifted towards a smaller mix of free family entertainment, concerts, local partnerships and community activities. The report also documents measurable participation in individual attractions—for example, concert ticket sales, museum attendance, recreation events and visitor surveys—suggesting a more granular accounting approach than simply applying a broad crowd estimate across downtown activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.roswell-nm.gov/1852/UFO-Festival-Report-2023" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: roswell-nm.gov">[Roswell NM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">roswell-nm.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Festival Report 2023</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell NMUFO Festival Report 2023 &#124; Roswell, NM...</span></span></span>

The comparison therefore combines two festivals that differed in scale, marketing emphasis and reporting inputs. Treating the two attendance figures as directly comparable headcounts risks overstating the apparent decline.

## What attendance and impact estimates really measure

One common misunderstanding is that "attendance" and "economic impact" describe the same phenomenon. They do not.

Attendance estimates may include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* unique out-of-town visitors;
* local residents attending events;
* participation across multiple venues;
* estimated crowds at free public activities.

</div>

Economic-impact estimates attempt to estimate visitor spending generated by the festival, including accommodation, food, entertainment and retail purchases. These calculations typically rely on tourism models, surveys and assumptions about visitor behaviour rather than direct measurement of every transaction. Roswell's reports explicitly present these figures as economic-impact estimates rather than audited financial totals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.roswell-nm.gov/DocumentCenter/View/24040" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: roswell-nm.gov">[Roswell NM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">roswell-nm.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell NMUFO FESTIVAL</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell NMUFO FESTIVAL</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because a festival can attract fewer people but generate relatively higher spending per visitor, or vice versa.

## Why published numbers should be interpreted cautiously

Roswell's published figures are useful indicators of tourism trends, but they should not be read as precise census-style counts.

Several factors contribute to year-to-year variation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Milestone anniversaries.** Round anniversaries receive significantly greater publicity and attract additional visitors.
* **Programming differences.** Headline concerts, celebrity guests, comic conventions and special exhibitions can substantially increase turnout.
* **Free versus ticketed events.** Open street activities require estimation techniques that differ from counting paid admissions.
* **Visitor methodology.** Survey-based estimates, ticket sales and economic models each capture different aspects of participation.
* **Weather and travel conditions.** Outdoor festivals remain sensitive to conditions that affect visitor behaviour.

</div>

Because many activities occur simultaneously across public streets, museums, parks and businesses, there is no single turnstile through which every attendee passes.

## Putting the figures into perspective

Longer-term reporting illustrates that Roswell's UFO Festival has never produced one stable attendance number. Earlier studies and reporting often cited crowds in the range of roughly 10,000 to 15,000 attendees, while city-commissioned economic studies have also produced varying estimates depending on methodology and the period examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://clutejournals.com/index.php/JBER/article/view/2356" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: clutejournals.com">[Clute Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">clutejournals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Clute JournalsBikers, Aliens, And Movie Stars: Comparing The Economic Impact Of Special Events &#124; Journal of Business &amp; Economics Research...</span></span></span>

The unusually high 2022 estimate should therefore be viewed as an exceptional anniversary benchmark rather than the normal baseline for future festivals. Likewise, the much lower 2023 estimate does not necessarily indicate a collapse in public interest. It reflects a combination of a non-milestone year, a different event structure and different measurement inputs.

For readers comparing [Roswell festival]({{ '1997-turning-point/' | relative_url }}) statistics across years, the most useful approach is to treat published attendance and economic-impact figures as context-dependent estimates. They are valuable for understanding broad tourism patterns, but they are not interchangeable audited headcounts and should always be interpreted alongside the scale, purpose and methodology of the specific year's festival report.
