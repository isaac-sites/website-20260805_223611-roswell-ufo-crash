## Introduction

The proposed **Project Mogul Flight 4** has become the pivotal event in the modern debate over whether [the Roswell debris]({{ 'the-roswell-debris/' | relative_url }}) can be explained by a classified balloon experiment rather than an extraterrestrial craft. Unlike many other Mogul launches, however, the evidence for Flight 4 is unusually fragmentary. Instead of a complete contemporary flight record, the case rests largely on a brief diary entry by geophysicist **Albert P. Crary**, later recollections by project engineer **Charles B. Moore**, and the US Air Force's 1994 reconstruction. This unusual documentary trail explains why the launch remains one of the most disputed pieces of the Roswell puzzle: supporters see it as the best match for the recovered debris, while critics argue that the surviving records are too incomplete and internally inconsistent to support such [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

## What Albert Crary recorded

The central primary source is Albert Crary's field diary for **4 June 1947**. His entry has become one of the most closely analysed documents in Roswell research because it appears to describe both a cancelled balloon operation and a limited launch on the same day.

Crary wrote that there were "no balloon flights again on account of clouds", but immediately followed this by noting that they "flew regular sonobuoy" in a cluster of balloons and obtained good reception on the ground but poor reception from an aircraft. He also mentioned firing explosive charges as part of the experiment. The apparent contradiction has generated decades of debate because the wording can be read either as [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) that no scheduled Mogul flight occurred or that only the planned constant-altitude flight was cancelled while a simpler equipment test proceeded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

The "regular sonobuoy" mentioned in the diary was not a naval anti-submarine deployment in the modern operational sense. Instead, it referred to an acoustic microphone package adapted for Project Mogul testing. Mogul's purpose was to determine whether low-frequency sound from distant nuclear explosions could be detected in the upper atmosphere, making acoustic sensors a logical component of experimental flights. The 4 June entry also records that the ground receiver performed well while airborne reception did not, indicating that the day's work focused on testing instrumentation rather than conducting a fully successful operational experiment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

A further complication is that [Flight 4]({{ 'flight-4/' | relative_url }}) does not appear in the surviving New York University summaries of constant-level balloon flights. Later researchers noted that these summaries excluded launches that merely tested equipment or failed before meaningful [altitude-control]({{ 'altitude-control/' | relative_url }}) experiments could begin. Consequently, the absence of Flight 4 from the formal reports is not, by itself, proof that nothing was launched.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/flight4.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo The wayward journey of NYU project flight #4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy Ufo The wayward journey of NYU project flight #4</span></span></span>

## Why the Air Force linked it to Roswell

When the US Air Force reinvestigated Roswell in the early 1990s, investigators concluded that the debris most likely originated from **Mogul Flight 4**. This conclusion depended on reconstructing the missing launch rather than discovering a complete flight log.

The Air Force argued that several factors made Flight 4 the strongest candidate:

* It occurred roughly one month before rancher W. W. "Mac" Brazel reportedly recovered unusual debris.
* Crary's diary indicated that a cluster of balloons carrying a sonobuoy had been launched despite poor weather.
* Charles [Moore]({{ 'moore/' | relative_url }}) recalled that this period involved experimentation with multiple radar targets and unusual balloon configurations.
* Only a small number of early June launches lacked complete recovery records, making Flight 4 the most plausible source of debris that could have remained undiscovered for days or weeks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

The Air Force also argued that eyewitness descriptions of foil, sticks, rubber and electronic components broadly matched equipment used in Mogul arrays. Although many individual parts resembled ordinary meteorological hardware, their combination into a long balloon train carrying experimental acoustic equipment would have been unfamiliar to personnel who were unaware of the project's classified objective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Importantly, the Air Force did not claim that a complete documentary record of Flight 4 existed. Instead, it acknowledged that the reconstruction relied on Crary's diary, surviving technical reports, witness recollections and engineering judgement about how the launch was probably configured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

## Why critics dispute the identification

The principal criticism is that the evidence supporting Flight 4 is largely inferential rather than documentary.

Critics point first to the wording of Crary's diary. The statement that there were "no balloon flights again on account of clouds" appears, on its face, to indicate cancellation. They argue that interpreting the following sentence as evidence for a significant Mogul launch requires assumptions about Crary's shorthand note-taking and later reconstruction of events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

A second objection concerns the missing paperwork. Unlike later Mogul operations, Flight 4 lacks a complete contemporary mission record describing its exact equipment, radar reflectors, flight path and recovery. The Air Force reconstruction therefore depends heavily on Charles Moore's recollections many decades after the event. While Moore had first-hand knowledge of the programme, critics question the reliability of memories reconstructed nearly fifty years later, especially when filling gaps in incomplete records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/flight4.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy Ufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy Ufo The wayward journey of NYU project flight #4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy Ufo The wayward journey of NYU project flight #4</span></span></span>

Some researchers also argue that if the 4 June launch consisted only of a simple cluster of meteorological balloons carrying a sonobuoy test package, it may not have included the elaborate radar reflector assemblies often cited as matching Roswell witness descriptions. Because the surviving documentation is incomplete, the precise configuration cannot be established with certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

## Why the missing flight remains central

The significance of the 4 June sonobuoy launch extends beyond a single balloon experiment. It illustrates the broader historical challenge of reconstructing classified Cold War programmes from incomplete archives.

If Crary's diary is interpreted as documenting a limited but genuine launch, Flight 4 offers a plausible mechanism by which classified balloon debris could have reached the Roswell area. If, however, the diary is interpreted as showing that the relevant launch never occurred, one of the Air Force's principal explanations for the Roswell debris becomes substantially weaker.

For that reason, the debate over Flight 4 is less about whether Project Mogul existed—which is well documented—than about whether this specific, poorly documented launch can legitimately be identified as the source of the debris recovered in New Mexico. The surviving evidence allows a plausible reconstruction but does not eliminate uncertainty, leaving the sonobuoy launch at the centre of one of the most enduring documentary disputes within the Roswell case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[lbl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>
