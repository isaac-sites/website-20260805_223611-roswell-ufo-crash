## Introduction

The leading Project Mogul explanation for [the Roswell debris]({{ 'the-roswell-debris/' | relative_url }}) depends heavily on a single question: could the balloon train commonly known as Flight 4 have drifted from its launch area near Alamogordo to the Foster ranch, where rancher W.W. Brazel found scattered debris? Because no complete tracking record survives for that flight, researchers did not simply follow a documented path. Instead, they reconstructed a probable route by combining surviving launch notes, weather records, upper-air wind data, recollections from Project Mogul personnel and the behaviour of neighbouring balloon flights. The result is not a confirmed flight path but a historically informed reconstruction intended to test whether the journey was physically plausible rather than to prove that it happened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[NSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report af roswell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REPORT OF AIR FORCE RESEARCH REGARDING THEMarch 24, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

## The proposed launch and last known observations

The reconstruction begins with one of the most disputed episodes in the Project Mogul record. The US Air Force's 1994 Roswell report identified a balloon launch on 4 June 1947 as the most likely source of the Roswell debris. That conclusion relied heavily on the field diary of geophysicist Albert P. Crary, which records activity on that date, including the release of a balloon cluster carrying a sonobuoy after planned operations were disrupted by cloud cover. The Air Force interpreted this launch as the operational flight later designated Flight 4, even though surviving documentation is incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[NSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report af roswell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REPORT OF AIR FORCE RESEARCH REGARDING THEMarch 24, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

The principal difficulty is that Flight 4 was never recovered by the New York University balloon team, leaving no direct record of its landing site. Unlike later flights that carried more comprehensive tracking equipment and generated better documentation, Flight 4 disappeared from the historical record shortly after launch. This absence of evidence made later reconstruction necessary rather than optional.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/ufo/report_af_roswell.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nsa.gov">[NSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nsa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report af roswell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REPORT OF AIR FORCE RESEARCH REGARDING THEMarch 24, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 24, 2018</span></span></span>

Researchers therefore had to infer where the balloon travelled by examining what is known about the launch conditions, the equipment believed to have been attached, and the behaviour of comparable Mogul flights conducted before and after 4 June.

## Reconstructing winds, altitude and direction

The most detailed reconstruction was undertaken by Charles B. [Moore]({{ 'moore/' | relative_url }}), one of the original Project Mogul engineers. After publication of the Air Force report, Moore revisited the surviving records to determine whether Flight 4 could realistically have reached the Roswell area. His objective was not to recreate every moment of the flight but to determine whether critics were correct in claiming that prevailing winds made such a journey impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.liquisearch.com/air_force_reports_on_the_roswell_ufo_incident/mogul_flight_4_reconstruction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liquisearch.com">[LiquiSearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liquisearch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liqui Search Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident - Mogul Flight 4 Reconstruction...</span></span></span>

Moore argued that simple surface wind measurements were inadequate because Mogul balloon trains climbed through several atmospheric layers before spending most of their flight in the lower stratosphere. In early June over New Mexico, winds near the ground could differ markedly from those several kilometres above. According to Moore's experience with the programme, summer stratospheric winds generally carried balloons eastward, meaning that ground-level observations alone could not eliminate the Foster ranch as a potential destination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.liquisearch.com/air_force_reports_on_the_roswell_ufo_incident/mogul_flight_4_reconstruction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liquisearch.com">[LiquiSearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liquisearch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liqui Search Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident - Mogul Flight 4 Reconstruction...</span></span></span>

To estimate the route, Moore combined several types of evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* surviving launch times recorded in Crary's diary;
* historical weather observations and upper-air wind measurements;
* tracking information preserved from Flights 5 and 6, which served as reference cases;
* knowledge of the ascent rate and operating altitude of Mogul balloon trains;
* corrections to apparent errors in the original tracking calculations, including adjustments involving magnetic declination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.liquisearch.com/air_force_reports_on_the_roswell_ufo_incident/mogul_flight_4_reconstruction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liquisearch.com">[LiquiSearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liquisearch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liqui Search Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident - Mogul Flight 4 Reconstruction...</span></span></span>

</div>

Rather than projecting a straight line across New Mexico, the reconstruction model estimated how long the balloon would spend climbing, when it would enter stronger upper-level winds and how those winds would alter its heading before eventual descent or structural failure. Because the [balloon train]({{ 'balloon-train/' | relative_url }}) consisted of numerous connected balloons and suspended equipment, its behaviour differed from that of a single weather balloon.

Moore concluded that a reconstructed trajectory brought Flight 4 close enough to the Foster ranch that it could not be dismissed as the source of the debris. Importantly, he presented this as a test of plausibility rather than an exact recreation of the flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.liquisearch.com/air_force_reports_on_the_roswell_ufo_incident/mogul_flight_4_reconstruction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: liquisearch.com">[LiquiSearch]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">liquisearch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Liqui Search Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Reports On The Roswell UFO Incident - Mogul Flight 4 Reconstruction...</span></span></span>

## What the route calculation can and cannot prove

The reconstructed flight path strengthened the Mogul explanation in one important respect: it showed that atmospheric conditions did not automatically rule out a balloon launched on 4 June reaching the Roswell region. ## Why the reconstruction remains significant

The Flight 4 reconstruction illustrates how historians and investigators address an event for which critical records are missing. Rather than relying on a single surviving document, the proposed route was assembled from independent strands of evidence: operational diaries, atmospheric science, engineering knowledge and comparison with neighbouring flights.

Its principal contribution is therefore modest but important. The reconstruction does not prove that Flight 4 landed on the Foster ranch, nor does it conclusively settle the Roswell debate. Instead, it addresses a narrower evidential question by showing that, based on the available meteorological and operational evidence, a [Project Mogul balloon]({{ 'project-mogul-balloon/' | relative_url }}) launched on 4 June 1947 could plausibly have travelled to the area where the debris was later reported.
