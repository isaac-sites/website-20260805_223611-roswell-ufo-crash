## Introduction

The U.S. Air Force did revisit the [Roswell incident]({{ 'the-1980-book/' | relative_url }}) in the 1990s, but it did **not** reopen Project Blue Book. This distinction is historically important because the two efforts had different purposes, methods and institutional contexts. Project Blue Book was an active Cold War programme that investigated contemporary reports of [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) flying objects between 1952 and 1969. The 1990s Roswell inquiry, by contrast, was a retrospective historical investigation launched almost 25 years after Blue Book had been closed and nearly 47 years after the original 1947 incident. Its task was to reconstruct events from surviving records, archived documents and witness interviews in response to congressional scrutiny rather than to investigate a new UFO case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Understanding that distinction helps explain why the 1990s reports reached different kinds of conclusions from Blue Book files. Rather than collecting fresh physical [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) or evaluating an ongoing aerial phenomenon, investigators attempted to determine what government records still existed, why some had disappeared, and whether surviving documentation could account for the reported debris recovery.

## What triggered the 1990s records review?

The immediate catalyst was not a renewed military concern about UFOs but a request from Congressman Steven H. Schiff of New Mexico. Constituents had asked Schiff to determine whether federal agencies still possessed records relating to the Roswell incident. In response, the U.S. Government Accountability Office (GAO), then called the General Accounting Office, began an audit in 1994 examining whether relevant government records existed and whether they had been handled properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

To support the GAO review, the Air Force undertook an extensive search of:

* Current Air Force offices.
* Military archives and federal records centres.
* Historical files relating to classified Cold War programmes.
* Surviving personnel who might have direct knowledge of the events.

</div>

The Secretary of the Air Force also released former personnel from security obligations where appropriate so they could speak more freely with investigators. This was an unusual historical records exercise rather than the reopening of an operational investigative unit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## How the historical reconstruction differed from a live UFO investigation

Although both Blue Book and the Roswell inquiry concerned unidentified aerial claims, they worked in fundamentally different ways.

Project Blue Book1990s Roswell inquiryInvestigated current UFO reports as they occurred.Examined a historical event nearly half a century later.Collected contemporary witness reports, photographs, radar data and military observations.Searched archives, reconstructed missing timelines and reviewed declassified records.Evaluated whether sightings posed a national security concern.Asked what the recovered debris most likely was based on surviving documentation.Closed permanently in December 1969.Conducted as a temporary investigation supporting a GAO inquiry.

Blue Book investigators generally worked with evidence available immediately after an event. By contrast, the Roswell team faced destroyed records, incomplete archives, fading memories and decades of accumulated public claims. Consequently, the inquiry relied heavily on documentary reconstruction rather than forensic investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why Blue Book itself was not reopened

Several practical and institutional reasons explain why the Air Force did not simply revive Project Blue Book.

First, Blue Book no longer existed. The programme had been terminated in 1969 after the Air Force concluded that UFO investigations had not demonstrated a threat to national security, advanced technology beyond contemporary scientific understanding or evidence of extraterrestrial vehicles. Its files had been transferred to the National Archives, making the project a historical rather than operational body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Second, Roswell was not being treated as a newly reported unidentified object. The central question was historical: what had happened in July 1947, and what documentary evidence remained? That required historians, archivists and records specialists rather than an active case-investigation office.

Third, the inquiry's legal framework differed. The GAO audit focused partly on federal records management—whether relevant documents had been preserved or destroyed under applicable procedures. That administrative question fell outside Blue Book's original mission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

## What the Air Force actually produced

Instead of reopening Blue Book, the Air Force published dedicated Roswell reports.

The first, *[The Roswell Report]({{ 'the-roswell-report/' | relative_url }}): Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert* (1995), argued that debris recovered in 1947 most likely originated from Project Mogul, a then-secret balloon programme designed to detect Soviet nuclear tests using long-range acoustic sensors. The report assembled newly available documentation on Mogul that had not been publicly accessible during the Cold War because of the programme's classified status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D301 PURL LPS71655</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Roswell Report: Fact Versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert - GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655 &#124; Content Details &#124; GovInfoJanuary 1...</span></span></span>

A second report, *The Roswell Report: Case Closed* (1997), addressed later claims concerning recovered bodies. ## Why the distinction changes the evidence

Confusing the 1990s inquiry with Blue Book can lead to misunderstandings about what evidence was actually examined.

A contemporary investigation normally evaluates fresh physical traces, immediate witness testimony and observations made close to the event. The Roswell inquiry had none of those advantages. By the 1990s:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Much original material had disappeared through routine records disposal or could not be located.
* Many witnesses had died or were recalling events from nearly five decades earlier.
* Public narratives had evolved through books, documentaries and repeated interviews.
* Previously classified Cold War programmes had become available for historical comparison.

</div>

As a result, the Air Force's conclusions rested primarily on documentary reconstruction rather than direct examination of original evidence. Even supporters and critics of the official explanation generally agree that this methodological difference shapes how much [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}) can be placed in various claims. Critics argue that missing records and inconsistencies leave important questions unresolved, while supporters contend that the newly declassified Project Mogul documentation provides the strongest documentary explanation available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[govinfo.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D301 PURL LPS71655</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Roswell Report: Fact Versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert - GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655 &#124; Content Details &#124; GovInfoJanuary 1...</span></span></span>

## What this means for understanding Roswell

The phrase "the Air Force reopened Roswell" is accurate only in a limited sense. The government reopened the historical question because Congress requested a records search, not because Project Blue Book resumed operations or because a new UFO investigation was launched.

The 1990s inquiry therefore belongs to a different category of investigation. It was a retrospective effort to explain a famous historical event using surviving archives and declassified Cold War records. Whether one accepts its conclusions or not, it should be understood as a records reconstruction conducted decades after Blue Book had ended, rather than as the continuation of the Air Force's Cold War UFO investigation programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
