## Introduction

By the mid-1990s, [the Roswell UFO]({{ 'the-roswell-ufo/' | relative_url }}) story had become so deeply embedded in popular culture that audiences no longer needed it explained. Instead of treating the alleged 1947 crash as a mystery, *Star Trek: Deep Space Nine* turned it into the premise for a comedy. Its 1995 episode **"Little Green Men"** imagines that the supposed extraterrestrials recovered near Roswell were not sinister invaders but three stranded Ferengi—Quark, Rom and Nog—whose accidental time travel places them at the centre of American UFO folklore. Rather than mocking the historical incident itself, the episode plays with the audience's familiarity with Roswell's established mythology, reversing expectations and using humour to expose how recognisable the legend had become.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Green_Men_%28Star_Trek%3A_Deep_Space_Nine%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Little Green Men (Star Trek: Deep Space Nine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Little Green Men (Star Trek: Deep Space Nine</span></span></span>

## The Ferengi as Roswell's recovered aliens

The episode begins with Quark purchasing a small spacecraft to transport his nephew Nog to Earth for Starfleet Academy. A mechanical failure throws the shuttle backwards through time to July 1947, where it crashes outside Roswell, New Mexico. The unconscious Ferengi are captured by the United States Army Air Forces, who naturally conclude they have discovered alien visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Green_Men_%28Star_Trek%3A_Deep_Space_Nine%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Little Green Men (Star Trek: Deep Space Nine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Little Green Men (Star Trek: Deep Space Nine</span></span></span>

This premise cleverly overlays established Roswell imagery with familiar *Star Trek* characters. Instead of mysterious [grey aliens]({{ 'grey-alien/' | relative_url }}), the "little green men" are Ferengi—already known to viewers as opportunistic traders obsessed with profit rather than conquest. The joke works because the audience recognises both myths simultaneously:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Roswell expects recovered alien bodies.
* *Star Trek* expects eccentric Ferengi behaviour.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Ferengi" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[memory-alpha.fandom.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* The collision of those expectations creates the comedy.

</div>

The script also adopts many visual conventions associated with Roswell stories and 1950s science fiction: military guards, interrogation rooms, scientists behind observation windows, nervous officers and secret hangars. The production deliberately imitates classic alien-invasion films while allowing the audience to know more than the characters on screen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Little_Green_Men_%28episode%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[Memory Alpha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memory Alpha Little Green Men (episode) &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memory Alpha Little Green Men (episode) &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

## How the episode reverses the human viewpoint

The episode's central comic device is perspective. Most Roswell fiction invites viewers to identify with frightened humans confronting unknown visitors. "Little Green Men" instead places viewers alongside the aliens.

From the Ferengi perspective, the humans appear irrational, primitive and unpredictable. When the universal translators fail, neither side understands the other. One memorable gag shows the Ferengi repeatedly striking their heads in an attempt to restart their translators; the observing military personnel assume this is an alien greeting and enthusiastically imitate them. The humour comes from mutual misunderstanding rather than hostility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Little_Green_Men_%28episode%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[Memory Alpha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memory Alpha Little Green Men (episode) &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memory Alpha Little Green Men (episode) &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

Other reversals continue throughout the episode:

* Human investigators become the bewildered subjects of observation.
* Military officials misinterpret ordinary Ferengi customs as [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) of alien behaviour.
* Quark attempts to exploit twentieth-century humans commercially instead of conquering them.
* Fear of extraterrestrial invasion is replaced by misunderstandings caused by language, culture and greed.

Even the threat of alien dissection—a staple of UFO folklore—is inverted. Here, it is the captured Ferengi who fear becoming experimental subjects in an American military laboratory, transforming a familiar horror trope into farce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Roswell_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[Memory Alpha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memory Alpha Roswell Incident &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memory Alpha Roswell Incident &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

## Why Roswell was ready for parody

The episode aired in November 1995, during a decade in which Roswell had returned to the centre of popular culture. Television series, books and documentaries had familiarised mainstream audiences with ideas such as recovered alien bodies, military cover-ups and secret government laboratories. Because these images were already widely recognised, *Deep Space Nine* did not need to establish the setting before subverting it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Green_Men_%28Star_Trek%3A_Deep_Space_Nine%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Little Green Men (Star Trek: Deep Space Nine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Little Green Men (Star Trek: Deep Space Nine</span></span></span>

Production history reflects this cultural moment. According to accounts from the series' production team, the concept had been suggested earlier but gained momentum as Roswell approached its fiftieth anniversary, when renewed public attention made the story especially timely. Rather than competing with increasingly serious conspiracy narratives, the writers chose to treat the mythology playfully.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Little_Green_Men_%28episode%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[Memory Alpha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memory Alpha Little Green Men (episode) &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memory Alpha Little Green Men (episode) &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

The episode also nods to earlier *Star Trek* time-travel adventures. Its title references the long-standing science-fiction image of "little green men", while the basic structure echoes *The Original Series* episode "Tomorrow Is Yesterday", in which Starfleet personnel also become entangled with twentieth-century American military authorities. These connections place Roswell within *Star Trek*'s own tradition of using historical settings to explore contemporary ideas through humour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Little_green_men" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[Memory Alpha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memory Alpha Little green men &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memory Alpha Little green men &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

## What the parody reveals about Roswell's cultural status

"Little Green Men" succeeds because it assumes viewers already understand the Roswell myth. The comedy depends on shared cultural knowledge rather than explanation. Audiences recognise the military base, the rumours of recovered spacecraft, the expectation of secret experiments and even the eventual official claim that only a weather balloon had been found.

The episode ultimately incorporates that famous explanation directly into its fictional history. After the Ferengi escape and return to their own century, the military suppresses the incident by announcing that the recovered object was merely a weather balloon—transforming the official historical account into the punchline of a *Star Trek* time-travel story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memory-alpha.fandom.com/wiki/Roswell" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memory-alpha.fandom.com">[Memory Alpha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memory-alpha.fandom.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memory Alpha Roswell &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memory Alpha Roswell &#124; Memory Alpha &#124; Fandom</span></span></span>

This approach illustrates how Roswell had evolved from a disputed historical event into a flexible cultural reference point. Earlier films often treated the alleged crash as hidden truth waiting to be uncovered. "Little Green Men" instead assumes the audience knows every familiar element of the legend and delights in rearranging them. The result is not a dismissal of Roswell but evidence of its extraordinary cultural familiarity: by 1995, the mythology was established enough that one of television's most successful science-fiction franchises could turn it into a sophisticated, affectionate comedy without losing the audience.
