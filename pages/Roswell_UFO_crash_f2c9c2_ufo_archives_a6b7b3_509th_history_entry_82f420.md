## Introduction

Among the surviving government records connected with the [Roswell incident]({{ 'the-1980-book/' | relative_url }}), the July 1947 history of the combined 509th Bomb Group and [Roswell Army Air]({{ 'roswell-army-air/' | relative_url }}) Field occupies a unique place. It is one of only two known federal documents created at the time of the event that directly mention the recovery, making it an important contemporary source. At the same time, its evidential value is often overstated. The entry confirms that personnel at Roswell dealt with an object initially described as a "flying disc" before military authorities concluded it was a radar-tracking balloon. It does not describe recovered bodies, an extraterrestrial craft, unusual materials or any secret recovery programme. Understanding both what the document says and what it does not say is essential to assessing its role in the historical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[gao.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124; U.S. GAOJuly 28, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 28, 1995</span></span></span>

## What the July 1947 entry actually records

The document forms part of the monthly historical report prepared for the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field. Rather than serving as an investigative report on a single incident, it summarises notable activities during July 1947 across the installation.

Its reference to Roswell is brief. The history notes that the Office of Public Information "was kept quite busy" answering enquiries about the "[flying disc]({{ 'flying-disc/' | relative_url }})", which had been reported to be in the possession of the 509th Bomb Group. It immediately adds that "the object turned out to be a radar tracking balloon." The passage occupies only a few lines within a much broader administrative report covering routine military business.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

Several aspects of this wording are significant:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It confirms that the base handled public enquiries following reports of a recovered "flying disc".
* It acknowledges that the 509th Bomb Group was publicly associated with the recovered object.
* It records the military's own contemporaneous conclusion that the object was a radar-tracking balloon.
* It presents the balloon identification as the settled outcome rather than as an unresolved question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

</div>

The report was certified by the commanding officer as a complete and accurate account of the month's activities, giving it greater evidential weight than later recollections recorded decades afterwards. ## What the phrase "flying disc" meant in context

Modern readers sometimes assume that the phrase "flying disc" in a 1947 military document implies official belief in an extraterrestrial craft. Contemporary usage was much broader.

During the summer of 1947, American newspapers routinely used "flying disc" or "flying saucer" as generic labels for unusual aerial reports following the widely publicised Kenneth [Arnold sighting]({{ 'arnold-sighting/' | relative_url }}) in June. The Roswell Army Air Field's initial press release likewise announced recovery of a "flying disc", reflecting the terminology then circulating in the press rather than a technical classification. Within a day, military officials publicly stated that the recovered material was from a balloon equipped with a radar reflector.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/roswell-ufo-aliens-what-happened" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[history.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">roswell ufo aliens what happened</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What Really Happened at Roswell? &#124; HISTORYDecember 18, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2019</span></span></span>

The wording in the monthly history mirrors that sequence. It records the public description first and then records the official explanation that followed. The document therefore preserves the chronology of events rather than endorsing the original identification.

## What the surviving entry does not support

The importance of the 509th history lies as much in its omissions as in its contents. Because it is a contemporaneous record, historians have examined it closely for [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) of later Roswell claims.

The document does **not** record:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* recovery of alien bodies;
* recovery of an extraterrestrial spacecraft;
* transport of exotic wreckage to secret facilities;
* extraordinary security measures beyond ordinary public-information activity;
* scientific examination of unfamiliar materials;
* references to intelligence cover-ups or special recovery teams.

</div>

None of these elements appear in the surviving July 1947 history. They entered the Roswell narrative decades later through witness testimony, books and subsequent interpretations rather than through this document itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

Equally important, the report does not claim to document every operational detail of the incident. As a monthly historical summary, it was never intended to preserve complete investigative records, communications logs or intelligence files.

## Why the document has clear evidential limits

The 509th history is a valuable primary source, but it cannot answer every question about Roswell because of its purpose and format.

First, it is extremely concise. The Roswell reference consists of only a short passage embedded within a report covering an entire month's activities. Its objective was administrative record-keeping rather than forensic documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

Second, it reflects the official understanding recorded by the base when the history was compiled. Whether that understanding was complete or mistaken is a separate historical question; the document itself simply records the conclusion reached by military authorities at that time.

Third, the broader documentary record is incomplete. The Government Accountability Office's investigation found that some Roswell Army Air Field administrative and outgoing message records had been destroyed under records-management practices, while other expected records could not be located. Those archival gaps mean historians cannot treat the surviving history as the entire documentary record of the incident. At the same time, missing records cannot by themselves be taken as evidence that extraordinary events occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

## How historians use the 509th history

The July 1947 history remains one of the strongest pieces of contemporaneous documentary evidence because it was written during the period in question rather than reconstructed years later. For that reason, it provides an important benchmark against which later claims can be compared.

Its evidential contribution is relatively narrow but well defined. It establishes that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a debris recovery generated widespread public interest;
* the recovered object was publicly described as a "flying disc";
* the 509th Bomb Group became associated with that recovery;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/1947f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[project1947.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
* the official military conclusion recorded in July 1947 identified the object as a radar-tracking balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

</div>

Beyond those points, the document neither confirms nor directly addresses the more elaborate claims that emerged in later decades. Consequently, it is best understood as a reliable contemporary administrative record with a deliberately limited scope, rather than as either definitive proof of the official explanation or evidence for the later allegations of recovered alien technology.
