## Introduction

The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) maintains the largest official public dataset of [modern unidentified]({{ 'modern-unidentified/' | relative_url }}) anomalous phenomena (UAP) reports assembled by the United States government. Rather than demonstrating a growing body of confirmed unexplained craft, the published case record shows three broad patterns: many reports can be identified as ordinary objects once sufficient data are available, a smaller number involve genuine aviation or national-security concerns, and a residual group remains unresolved because the available evidence is too incomplete for a reliable conclusion. This distinction is central to the modern UAP debate and to wider discussions surrounding the [Roswell UFO crash]({{ 'roswell-ufo-crash/' | relative_url }}). AARO's published findings treat "unidentified" as a statement about the quality of available evidence rather than proof of an extraordinary origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

## How the case totals were assembled

AARO's annual reporting combines reports from military personnel, intelligence organisations and other government sources. Cases are not limited to newly observed events. Each reporting cycle may also include historical incidents that were submitted late or transferred from earlier databases, meaning the total number of reports is not the same as the number of new sightings during a single year. Cases are reviewed as additional radar tracks, imagery, flight records, weather information and intelligence become available, allowing some reports to move from unresolved to resolved over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

Across its public reporting, AARO has accumulated approximately 1,870 reports for analysis. Importantly, this figure reflects reports received rather than unique anomalous objects. Multiple observers may report the same event, while some reports lack enough information even to establish whether a single object was involved. The case record is therefore best understood as an operational reporting database rather than a catalogue of verified unknown vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

## What resolved cases were identified as

The strongest pattern in AARO's published dataset is that resolved cases consistently receive conventional explanations. As additional [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) is collected, reports have been attributed to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Commercial and military aircraft.
* Balloons, including research and weather balloons.
* Uncrewed aerial systems (drones).
* Birds and other airborne wildlife.
* Satellites and other space objects.
* Atmospheric effects or optical phenomena.
* Sensor artefacts, processing errors or misinterpretation of infrared imagery.

</div>

Successive reporting periods have produced the same overall trend. When AARO announces that cases have been resolved, the identified causes overwhelmingly fall into familiar categories rather than previously unknown technology. This finding mirrors earlier government investigations stretching back through Project Blue Book and other official reviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

Some reports nevertheless remain operationally significant. Even when an object ultimately proves ordinary, an [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) drone operating near military facilities or training ranges may represent an intelligence or air-safety concern until it is identified. AARO therefore treats rapid identification as both a scientific and national-security task.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why unresolved cases are not confirmed anomalies

A common misunderstanding is that unresolved cases represent evidence of extraordinary technology. AARO explicitly rejects that interpretation. According to its assessments, most unresolved reports lack critical information such as accurate range, altitude, speed, sensor calibration, environmental conditions or multiple independent observations. Without these data, investigators cannot confidently identify the object—but neither can they justify extraordinary conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>

NASA's independent UAP study reached a similar conclusion. It found that current reports are often limited by inconsistent data quality and recommended better calibrated sensors, richer metadata and coordinated observations before strong scientific conclusions can be drawn. ## What the dataset says—and does not say

The accumulated AARO record supports several evidence-based conclusions.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AAARO_Historical_Record_Report_Volume_1_2024.pdf/7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[en.wikisource.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Page:AARO Historical Record Report Volume</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO is committed to reaching conclusions based on empirical evidence. <div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The reporting system captures genuine observations made by trained personnel and should not be dismissed as fabrication.
* Most cases that receive enough supporting information are eventually attributed to ordinary objects or known phenomena.
* Some reports remain unresolved because investigators lack sufficient evidence, not because they have confirmed exotic capabilities.
* AARO continues to assess unresolved reports for possible foreign intelligence collection, aviation hazards or other national-security implications.
* The published case record has not produced verified evidence of extraterrestrial technology or recovered non-human craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf?emci=622cd777-b774-f011-8dc9-6045bda9d96b&amp;emdi=ea000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001&amp;sourceid=1070813" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unclassified Final DSD AARO Historical ReportMarch 8, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2024</span></span></span>
