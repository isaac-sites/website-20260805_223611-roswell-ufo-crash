## Introduction

Project Blue Book is often assumed to be the definitive archive for every major American UFO incident. That assumption leads many people to ask why the July 1947 [Roswell incident]({{ 'the-1980-book/' | relative_url }}) is missing from its files. The short answer is that Roswell was never processed as a standard Blue Book case. By the time the Air Force established the investigative systems that eventually became Project Blue Book, military authorities had already classified the Roswell recovery as a resolved debris incident rather than an unresolved [unidentified]({{ 'unidentified/' | relative_url }}) flying object report. As a result, the case did not enter the later catalogue of sightings that Blue Book maintained. This absence is well documented by the US National Archives, but it does not by itself demonstrate either a cover-up or the destruction of a complete Blue Book investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Blue Book was the final stage in a succession of Air Force UFO programmes that began with Project Sign in 1948 and Project Grudge before being renamed Project Blue Book in 1952. Its purpose was to collect, evaluate and archive reports of unidentified aerial sightings submitted by military personnel and civilians. Investigators assessed witness statements, photographs, radar reports, weather information and other [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) to determine whether a sighting could be explained.

The programme ultimately investigated 12,618 reported sightings between 1947 and 1969, with 701 remaining officially categorised as unidentified. The surviving records consist largely of case files organised by date and location, along with administrative files, photographs, films and supporting documents. These materials are now held by the US National Archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Importantly, Blue Book was not designed as a comprehensive archive of every military event that later became associated with UFO claims. It primarily documented reports that entered its investigative system.

## How Roswell differed from a standard sighting case

The Roswell incident followed a different administrative path from the outset.

In July 1947 personnel from [Roswell Army Air]({{ 'roswell-army-air/' | relative_url }}) Field recovered debris from a ranch in New Mexico. After an initial press release describing the recovery of a "[flying disc]({{ 'flying-disc/' | relative_url }})", senior military officials quickly announced that the material was consistent with a weather balloon and radar target. Regardless of whether later researchers accept that explanation, the key administrative point is that the incident was treated by the military as a resolved recovery rather than an ongoing unidentified aerial sighting requiring later investigation through Project Sign, Grudge or Blue Book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That distinction mattered because Blue Book's filing system centred on reports submitted for evaluation after the investigative programme was operating. Roswell was already considered closed before those later procedures became established.

In other words, Roswell did not disappear from Blue Book because someone removed a completed Blue Book case file. Instead, the available evidence indicates that it never became a Blue Book case in the first place.

## What the National Archives actually found

The National Archives has directly addressed this question because it receives frequent enquiries from researchers.

Its guidance on Project Blue Book states that extensive searches of the Blue Book records have failed to locate documentation concerning the July 1947 Roswell incident. That statement refers specifically to the Blue Book collection itself, not to every federal record relating to Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction is significant because Roswell-related material exists elsewhere. During the 1990s, at the request of Congress and as part of the Air Force's investigation into Roswell, officials assembled historical records, interviews and supporting documentation that are separate from the original Blue Book files. The National Archives now preserves these [Roswell Report]({{ 'the-roswell-report/' | relative_url }}) source files as their own archival series alongside—but distinct from—the Blue Book collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the missing file can—and cannot—prove

The absence of a Roswell file in Project Blue Book is often interpreted in sharply different ways, but the archival evidence supports a more limited conclusion.

### What it does show

The missing case is consistent with how the Air Force organised its investigative records. If Roswell was considered resolved before the later UFO reporting system matured, there would be no expectation that it would appear among Blue Book's standard sighting files.

The National Archives' inability to locate a Roswell Blue Book case therefore aligns with the documented purpose and structure of the programme rather than contradicting it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

### What it does not show

Equally, the absence of a Blue Book file is not proof that all Roswell records survived intact, nor is it proof that an extraterrestrial recovery file was deliberately removed.

Government investigations in the 1990s acknowledged that some administrative records from Roswell Army Air Field had been destroyed under routine records-management practices over subsequent decades. However, investigators did not report evidence that a Blue Book Roswell case had existed and was later concealed. Instead, they concluded that no such case file could be found because the incident had not entered that investigative system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

For historians, this distinction is important. Missing records and missing case files are not the same thing.

## Why the distinction still matters

The Roswell debate often assumes that Project Blue Book functioned as a master index for every government encounter with unidentified objects. The surviving documentation shows a narrower reality.

Blue Book was an investigative programme with defined procedures, jurisdiction and filing practices. Roswell originated before those procedures became established and was administratively handled as a recovered object whose identity had, according to the military's official position at the time, already been determined. Consequently, later archivists inherited thousands of Blue Book sighting investigations but no corresponding Roswell case within that collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The absence of a Roswell file is therefore best understood as evidence of how the Air Force organised its records rather than as independent evidence either supporting or disproving extraordinary claims about what happened near Roswell in July 1947.
